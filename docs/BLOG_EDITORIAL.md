# InstaCal editorial brief

## The promise

Help someone understand food and fitness, try something useful, and see why InstaCal belongs in their routine. Write like a knowledgeable friend with good judgment. Be specific, inviting, concise and original. A compelling hook must survive checking the underlying evidence.

## Brand

InstaCal is the social app for food and fitness: share meals, discover ideas, track nutrition and workouts, and bring friends along. Connect naturally to a verified product behavior. Do not invent features, testimonials, credentials, success stories or health outcomes. Avoid repeated download pitches in every section; the article template includes a product CTA.

## Evidence

- Prefer primary research, NIH, CDC, USDA and recognized professional guidance. Open sources before using them.
- Cite numerical, nutrition, food-safety and health claims near the relevant section. Store access dates and a readable source name.
- Community aggregates describe app logs, not complete intake or the general population. Include dates, units, sample sizes, exclusions and weighting. Different contributor pools are not longitudinal results.
- Do not claim causation, clinical significance, weight loss or muscle gain from app logs. Do not market ordinary meals as cures or universal prescriptions.
- Use at least 20 contributors and 100 records for every public group. Do not identify people or publish rare slices. Never query private health records for editorial material.
- Never invent keyword volume, food macros, recipes recovered from an image, preparation times, national trends or research citations.
- Original meal ideas are allowed when identified as editorial suggestions. A tested recipe claim requires an actual test. Nutrition totals require ingredient quantities, a specific source/label and a stated serving yield.
- Food safety claims need current authoritative guidance. Weight and training articles should be general adult education, avoiding extreme restrictions, shame and guaranteed results.

## Visuals

Use actual public member photos with verified eligibility and a clear caption. The user questioned AI cover generation, and we switched to real photos. No AI-generated food, bodies or workout scenes. Do not retouch a photograph into a misleading account of the meal. Charts are built from actual query values, start at zero where appropriate and explain units and denominators.

## SEO that earns the click

Choose one useful primary query and 3–6 supporting phrases. Write a specific title and clear description (50–180 characters). Answer the reader's question early. Use descriptive headings and genuinely useful FAQ answers; no keyword stuffing, word-count padding or mass rewriting of other sites. Link to related existing articles when appropriate. The website supplies canonical URLs, article metadata, JSON-LD, sitemap and RSS.

## Article structure

1. Hook with a concrete everyday problem.
2. Two to five takeaways.
3. Explain the evidence, with caveats next to the numbers.
4. Give concrete examples or a usable table/framework.
5. Give the reader one next step and a natural InstaCal connection.
6. Answer real follow-up questions.
7. List sources, dates, methodology and limitations.

Byline: InstaCal Editorial, with transparent AI-assisted writing disclosure. Do not call content medically reviewed unless an actual named professional reviewed it.
